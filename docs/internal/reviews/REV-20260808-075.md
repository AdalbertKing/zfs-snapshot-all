# REV-20260808-075 — local relation contract activates cron before proving the first seed

## Verdict

**APPROVED.**

The implementation at `7bc80a4875374c4aa85bd90a2f2e9240216af613` corrects the contract before code exists. The durable ordering is now:

```text
configured
  -> foreground seed succeeds
seeded
  -> managed cron install succeeds and is read back
active
```

A failed seed leaves `configured` with no managed local cron. A cron install/read-back failure leaves `seeded`, not `active`. Retry from `configured` repeats the seed; retry from `seeded` retries activation without repeating a proven seed unless explicitly requested.

Independent inspection of `lib-cron.sh` confirms the contract may rely on the existing writer: mutation is serialized by the shared per-user `flock`, the read-modify-write happens under that lock, and `cron_write()` re-reads the crontab after `crontab(1)` and compares it before reporting success. The documented limitation regarding a concurrent human `crontab -e` is pre-existing and is not introduced by the local-relation feature.

Later owner-driven CLI revisions do not change this state/side-effect invariant. They replace the proposed public spelling and retry UX, not the required ordering.

## Original finding — F1 — P1

The original draft installed cron before the first foreground seed. That could expose scheduled jobs on a relationship that had never completed bootstrap and could race a long seed. The correction moved cron activation after successful seed and aligned persistent states with real side effects.

## Verification decision

This review covered a documentation/contract correction only. No live ZFS campaign is required to approve the contract change itself. When the local runtime is implemented, its acceptance still requires targeted automated state-transition tests plus one real scratch cross-pool proof that an induced seed failure leaves no managed cron, followed by successful seed/activation and crontab/state read-back.
