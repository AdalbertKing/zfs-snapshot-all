# Response to REV-20261023-170

## F1 — IMPLEMENTED

### Analysis

Accepted as stated. `restore_grant_namespaces` maps every scope on a pool to the same
`<pool>/restore`, and ZFS stores one permission set per `(dataset, account)`. Two
relationships to one peer normally share its `zfsbackup-<peer>` account, so two grants on
disjoint scopes of one pool hold the same tuple. `do_deny_restore` ran the namespace
`zfs unallow` unconditionally. Withdrawing the first grant therefore removed the
capability the second, still active grant needed, and it returned 0, so nothing
revealed the break.

### Planned or implemented change

`deploy.sh` only. The REV-147 order (record moved first, unallow, resume only
after a proven revocation) is unchanged.

- **New `restore_ns_other_owners <label> <account> <namespace>`.** It lists every
  OTHER grant that still needs the namespace: those standing in `RESTORE_GRANT_DIR`
  and those still being revoked in `RESTORE_REVOKE_DIR`. A grant counts when it
  has the same account and its scope maps to that namespace.
  - Its account comes from its record (`RESTORE_GRANT_ACCOUNT`, or
    `RESTORE_REVOKE_ACCOUNT` on a pending revocation), otherwise from its
    pairing manifest.
  - A grant whose account cannot be told keeps nothing alive. Another account's
    tuple is neither retained nor revoked by guesswork.
- **The namespace loop in `do_deny_restore`** skips the `unallow` while another
  owner remains, and logs `kept: … still needed by grant(s): <labels>`. The last
  owner unallows as before, and before its grant-owned writers resume. A failed
  unallow keeps the REV-147 fail-closed retry.
- **New grants record their account.** `do_allow_restore` writes
  `RESTORE_GRANT_ACCOUNT`, so the accounting does not depend on a manifest that
  may change later. Grants written before this fall back to the manifest.
- Different accounts and different pools stay isolated, because the tuple
  compared is `(namespace, account)`.

### Remaining risk

- A grant written before this change, whose pairing manifest is gone, has no
  readable account. It does not keep a shared namespace alive, so the namespace
  could be revoked under it, as before. Every grant written from now on carries
  its account.
- The per-dataset scope `unallow` is unchanged. Two grants of one account with
  OVERLAPPING scopes would share those tuples too. The review covers disjoint
  scopes, and the remaining Restore permission item (#550) is out of this REV, as the
  handoff says.
- No live ZFS: the regression uses the fake permission set, like the review's
  reproducer.
