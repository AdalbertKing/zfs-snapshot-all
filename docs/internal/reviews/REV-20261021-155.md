## F1 — P1, blocking enablement: numeric but out-of-contract thresholds exhaust the candidate list

- Affected: `hostscripts/capacity-guard.sh`, configuration validation at lines
  46–50 and destructive loop at lines 319–350.
- Evidence: `AT`, `TARGET` and `KEEP` reject non-digits, but do not enforce the
  public bounds. In particular, `guard_at=0` and `guard_target=0` pass parsing;
  line 50 rewrites the target to `-1`. No real capacity can become less than
  `-1`, so the loop destroys every otherwise eligible snapshot and only then
  exits 1. A nonzero exit does not restore deleted snapshots.
- Executable reproduction:
  `bash docs/internal/reviews/evidence/REV-20261021-155-capacity-guard.sh`.
  On this exact main, its isolated fake-ZFS case returns `rc=1`, performs three
  `zfs destroy` calls, and removes every eligible point; the disabled and valid
  controls demonstrate that the harness is exercising the shipped script.
- Impact: a malformed manual setting, environment override or legacy config
  turns a bounded emergency prune into maximum permitted deletion. F10's
  registry constrains its own editor, but the script explicitly supports all
  three configuration channels and must reject unsafe values at the destructive
  boundary.

### Required outcome and acceptance criteria

1. Validate the effective values before pool enumeration or any `zfs destroy`:
   `guard_at` within its supported percentage range, `guard_target` within its
   supported range and strictly below `guard_at`, and `guard_min_keep` within
   its supported positive range. Do not silently coerce an invalid destructive
   policy into another policy.
2. Invalid/out-of-range values from environment, `settings.ini` or legacy conf
   must perform zero destructive calls, return nonzero, and emit a diagnostic
   and alert naming the bad setting. Cover boundary values as well as `0/0`.
3. Keep the documented precedence order; a lower-priority valid value must not
   silently replace an explicitly supplied invalid higher-priority value.

## F2 — P1, blocking enablement: failed `zpool sync` is ignored and deletion continues

- Affected: `hostscripts/capacity-guard.sh`, lines 323–334.
- Evidence: after each successful destroy, `zpool sync "$pool"` discards its
  status. The script then reads capacity and continues through the precomputed
  candidate list. The same evidence script makes every sync fail: current code
  still executes all three eligible destroys (`rc=1`, three destroys), rather
  than stopping after the first failed synchronization.
- Impact: the contract says capacity is synchronized and measured after every
  deletion. When synchronization fails, the guard has lost the prerequisite
  for deciding whether another irreversible deletion is necessary. Continuing
  can remove the entire eligible history during a pool/control-path fault.

### Required outcome and acceptance criteria

1. Treat a failed `zpool sync` as a hard stop for that pool: no subsequent
   `zfs destroy` calls in the run, nonzero result, explicit log and alert. The
   already completed deletion cannot be undone and must remain listed.
2. Treat an unreadable/non-numeric post-delete capacity measurement the same
   way; do not continue from a stale or unknown value.
3. Add a regression with at least two candidates proving that failure after
   the first deletion never reaches the second candidate, plus a healthy
   control that still stops below `guard_target`.
